On a Wednesday morning in Almasi Woods, Garden Estate, the bathroom we walked into was
bare plaster with a white undercoat. There was no tiling, no finish and nothing to suggest
what it would become. By Saturday it was a finished shower room: walls, floor, shower tray
and a built-in vanity, all in one continuous surface with no tiles or grout lines.

This is the story of how that room came together, and of the installer whose hands did
most of the work.

## The Brief: One Surface, No Grout

The homeowner wanted a bathroom that felt calm and solid, and that would stay that way.
That ruled out the usual answer. Tiles look sharp on handover day, but a tiled bathroom is
really hundreds of small surfaces held together by grout. In a shower, grout is where
discolouration and mould start.

Mixx Cement removes that problem. It is a decorative micro-cement that goes on in thin
coats and cures into a single seamless skin, so there are no joints to clean or crack.
(If you're new to the material, our explainer
[What Is Mixx Cement?](/blog/what-is-mixx-cement/) covers the basics.)

## The Materials

Every Mixx job starts with three things on site, and this one was no different.

![A 5-litre container of Mixx Neutral Binder beside a bag of Mixx plaster marked White Linen](/assets/blog/almasi-woods-shower/neutral-binder-white-linen.jpg)

- **Mixx plaster in White Linen.** This is a soft, warm off-white. Under natural light it
  reads cream rather than stark white, which keeps a small bathroom from feeling clinical.
- **Neutral Binder.** It is mixed into the plaster to strengthen the bond and improve
  flexural and abrasive strength, and it helps limit shrinkage as the coat dries.
- **Supreme Seal.** This is a two-part polyurethane sealer (Part A plus a Part B
  activator) made for wet areas. In a shower, the sealer does the hard work: it closes the
  surface against water and makes everyday cleaning a quick wipe.

![The two-part Mixx Supreme Seal: a Part A bucket with the Part B activator tin on top](/assets/blog/almasi-woods-shower/supreme-seal.jpg)

## Wednesday to Saturday

Starting from bare plaster made the job straightforward: there were no tiles to chip off,
no rubble to remove and no making good after demolition. The surface was prepared, and
the Mixx went straight on.

The work followed the normal Mixx sequence:

1. **Prepare.** The plastered and undercoated surfaces were checked, cleaned and made
   ready to take the coating.
2. **Build the coats.** The White Linen plaster, mixed with Neutral Binder, was trowelled
   on in thin coats across the walls, floor, shower area and vanity. Each coat was allowed
   to dry before the next.
3. **Seal.** The finished surfaces were sealed, with Supreme Seal protecting the wet zones.

Four working days, Wednesday to Saturday, took the room from undercoat to finished.

## The Finished Room

### A shower without a single joint

![The finished Mixx Cement shower floor, falling to a stainless-steel drain with no grout lines](/assets/blog/almasi-woods-shower/shower-floor-drain.jpg)

The shower floor, walls and upstand form one unbroken surface that falls towards the
drain. Where a tiled shower would have dozens of grout lines collecting soap scum, this
has none. The finish carries straight through the corners, so there are no joints for
water to work into.

### A curved privacy wall

The shower is screened by a half-height wall with a sweeping curved top. This is the kind
of shape that is awkward and wasteful to tile, because every tile along the curve has to be
cut. Micro-cement simply follows the form, wrapping around the curve without a break.

### A vanity with character

![A built-in Mixx Cement vanity with a basin cut-out and small hand-painted gecko and longhorn tile insets](/assets/blog/almasi-woods-shower/vanity-tile-insets.jpg)

The built-in vanity is the detail people notice. Its top, front and sides are finished in
the same White Linen micro-cement as the room around it. Small hand-painted tile insets of
geckos and a longhorn are set into the surface, adding a playful touch to an otherwise
quiet room.

### One continuous floor

![The finished bathroom floor in Mixx Cement running seamlessly from the doorway to the vanity](/assets/blog/almasi-woods-shower/finished-floor-vanity.jpg)

The floor runs from the doorway, past the vanity and into the shower without a threshold
strip or change of material. The sealed surface has a soft sheen that catches the light
from the window and makes the room feel larger than it is.

## Living With It

The sealed surface needs very little care. Day to day it needs only a wipe-down with
warm water and a mild, pH-neutral cleaner. There's no grout to scrub and no tiles to come
loose. Harsh abrasives and strong acidic or bleach-based cleaners should be avoided because
they wear the sealer over time.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can Mixx Cement be used inside a shower?

Yes. In wet areas Mixx is sealed with Supreme Seal, a two-part polyurethane sealer
designed for showers and other surfaces that are regularly exposed to water.

### How long does a Mixx bathroom take?

It depends on the size of the room and the condition of the surfaces. This bathroom in
Almasi Woods went from bare plaster to a finished, sealed shower room in four days.

### Does Mixx need to go onto bare plaster?

No. Bare plaster is a good starting point, but Mixx also bonds directly onto existing
tiles, so many bathrooms can be refinished without removing the old tiles first.

### What colour is this bathroom?

This project used White Linen, a warm off-white from the Mixx colour range. Other colours
are available, and finished samples can be seen at our showroom.
