Modular construction in Kenya is usually sold as a shortcut. It is not one. It is a
different sequence — most of the building is made to a fixed dimension before anyone
reaches site, and site work becomes assembly rather than fabrication.

That distinction is what lets a client be given a programme and a price that both hold.
Here is what the method involves, and how Pavilion Master Builders delivers it as one
turnkey package rather than a chain of separate trades.

## What modular construction actually means

Conventional wet construction makes the building on site. Blocks are laid, mortar cures,
plaster goes on wet and must dry before the next trade starts. Every one of those steps
depends on weather, and each absorbs its own delay.

Dry, modular construction inverts it. Components are produced to dimension off site,
delivered in sequence, and fixed rather than cast. Waste drops because material is cut to
a list, and quality control moves somewhere it can be measured.

The commercial consequence matters more than the technical one. When fabrication is
controlled, cost becomes predictable — variation comes from design change, not from what
the site did that week.

## Light gauge steel construction in Kenya

The structural system is [light gauge steel framing](/products/light-gauge-steel):
cold-formed sections rolled to a specific design, pre-punched for services and labelled
for assembly.

For a specifier the attraction is dimensional consistency. Members arrive at the designed
length, so the tolerance-chasing that consumes conventional framing does not arise and
follow-on trades inherit a square frame. Service routes are already punched, so first fix
does not wait on drilling.

Sections are specified against the loading and spans of each project rather than taken
from a standard catalogue.

The trade-off is front-loaded design: because members are manufactured to a resolved
drawing, late changes carry engineering cost in a way they do not with in-situ framing.
That discipline is what produces the programme certainty.

## Fiber cement cladding and the dry envelope

The envelope follows the same logic. [Fiber cement boards](/products/fiber-cement) fix
directly to the steel frame, so the building is closed without curing time and without a
wet trade sitting on the critical path.

The material resists fire, termites and weather, removing several of the failure modes
that shorten a façade's life on humid or coastal sites. Boards are cut to size and fixed
at set centres, so the envelope goes on at a predictable rate.

Because frame and board are both dimensionally stable, junctions can be detailed rather
than made good on site. That is where dry construction earns its durability — in the
interfaces being designed before anyone builds them.

## Roofing and finishes, in plain terms

Roofing is where most owners' questions start. [Decra stone coated roofing
tiles](/products/decra-roofing) are steel tiles with a stone chip surface, and the roof we
fit most often in Kenya.

The appeal is simple. They are light, so they suit a steel frame and do not load the
structure. They are weatherproof. And they are built to last fifty years and more.

Inside, [Mixx decorative micro-cement](/products/mixx-cement) gives walls and floors a
smooth, seamless finish with no tiles and no grout lines. It goes over the same fiber
cement boards used elsewhere, so a wall can be framed, clad and finished without
switching system. Nothing to re-grout, nothing to work loose.
