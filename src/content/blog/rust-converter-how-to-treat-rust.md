Rust doesn't wait. One rainy season and the gate that looked fine in January has orange streaks running down it by May. Roof trusses, window grilles, water tank stands, shipping containers, farm equipment and vehicle chassis all suffer the same way, and along the coast the salty air makes it happen even faster.

The usual fix is hours of grinding and wire-brushing to get back to bare metal, then priming and painting. There's an easier way. A rust converter treats the rust where it is and turns it into a stable surface you can paint over.

This guide covers what causes rust, the three main ways to treat it, and exactly how to apply a rust converter so the job lasts.

## What causes rust?

Rust is iron oxide. It forms when iron or steel is exposed to water and oxygen together. Rain, humidity, condensation and salt all speed it up. Once rust starts, it's porous and flaky, so it holds moisture against the metal and keeps eating deeper. Painting straight over loose rust doesn't stop it. The rust keeps spreading underneath until the paint bubbles and peels off.

To stop rust for good, you have to deal with the rust that's already there before you paint.

## 3 ways to treat rust on metal

| Method | How it works | Best for | Downsides |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Mechanical removal** (grinding, sanding, sandblasting) | Physically strips rust back to bare metal | New fabrication, small parts | Slow, noisy, dusty; hard to reach corners and pitting; bare metal rusts again quickly if not primed |
| **Rust remover** (gel or liquid) | Chemically dissolves rust | Restoring tools, parts, heavily corroded items you want back to bare metal | Rinsing and neutralising needed; metal must be primed straight away |
| **Rust converter** | Chemically changes the rust into a stable, paintable layer | Gates, grilles, structures, vehicles, containers, machinery, anything too big or awkward to strip | Needs some rust present to work; not for non-ferrous metals |

For most real-world jobs, such as a rusty gate, a steel structure or a truck chassis, a rust converter saves the most time and labour.

## What is a rust converter?

A rust converter is a liquid you brush onto rusted steel or iron. Instead of removing the rust, it reacts with it chemically and turns it into a hard, dark, stable layer that's sealed against moisture. That layer acts as a primer, so you can paint straight over it.

In short: you don't have to remove all the rust. You convert it.

## How to apply a rust converter, step by step

Follow these steps for a result that lasts. They come from the manufacturer's instructions for Neutrarust 661.

### 1. Prepare the surface

- Wire-brush off flaky rust, loose scale and dirt.
- Knock down any high rust peaks.
- Scrape blistered paint back to hard rust.
- Leave the hard, tight rust in place. The converter needs it to react with.
- If the surface has grease, oil or chemicals on it, wash it with detergent and rinse with clean water. Let it dry.

### 2. Prepare the product

- Stir well. Do not dilute with water, thinners or spirits.
- Pour what you need into a plastic (non-metallic) container. Never pour unused product back into the tin, because rust particles will contaminate it.

### 3. Apply 2–3 thin coats

- Brush or roll it on, working it well into pits and corners. Cross-brush for the best result.
- It goes on white. Keep brushing gently for about 30 seconds more, then leave it to dry. As it converts, the rust turns dark and ready for paint.
- Leave about 2 hours between coats. It's touch dry in roughly 20 minutes in hot weather and about 45 minutes in cool weather.
- Wipe off any drips or splashes on nearby paintwork straight away, before they dry and stain.

### 4. Let it cure, then paint

- Leave it 24–48 hours to dry hard before applying primer or topcoat. Allow longer in cool or damp weather.
- Keep it out of the rain while it dries.
- Finish with a good-quality primer, then your topcoat. Test paint compatibility on a small area first.

**Working conditions:** apply only when the metal surface is between 5°C and 40°C. Avoid painting in strong direct sunlight, because metal left in the midday sun can get much hotter than the air.

## Where NOT to use it

- **Galvanised steel, aluminium, copper or stainless steel.** These are non-ferrous or coated metals, and there's no iron rust for the converter to react with.
- **Over existing sound paint.** Scrape back to the rust first.
- **Surfaces that get hotter than 60°C,** such as engines, boilers, braai stands or stoves.
- **Clean, rust-free metal.** Use a proper metal primer instead.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **Grinding off all the rust first.** The converter needs rust to work.
- **Thinning the product.** It's designed to be used neat.
- **Applying one thick coat.** Two or three thin coats convert better and dry properly.
- **Painting too soon.** Give it 24–48 hours to cure.
- **Leaving it unpainted outdoors.** The converted layer is a primer, so a topcoat gives it long-term protection.

## How much rust converter do I need?

A rough guide: **area in m² ÷ 12 × number of coats = litres needed.** Add extra for heavily pitted surfaces.

Example: a steel gate with 6 m² per side has 12 m² to cover. With 2–3 coats that's about 2–3 litres, so a 5-litre tin covers it comfortably with some left over for touch-ups.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can you paint over a rust converter?

Yes, that's the whole point. Once it has dried hard (24–48 hours), the converted surface makes a good base for primer and most paint systems.

### Rust converter vs rust remover: what's the difference?

A rust remover dissolves rust to get you back to bare metal. A rust converter leaves the rust in place and turns it into a stable, paintable layer. For large or hard-to-reach steelwork, a converter saves a lot of time.

### Does rust converter work on mabati or galvanised sheets?

Not on galvanised coating. The converter only reacts with iron rust on ferrous metal. On galvanised surfaces, use a primer made for galvanised metal.

### How long does it take to work?

Each coat is touch dry in about 20–45 minutes depending on the weather, and you'll see the rust turn dark as it converts. Allow 2 hours between coats and 24–48 hours before painting.

### Is Neutrarust 661 safe to use?

It's water-based, contains no mineral acids, and is classified as non-hazardous for transport. As with any coating, wear gloves and eye protection, work in a well-ventilated area, and keep it away from children, food and drinking water.

### How long does a tin last once opened?

Use it within 12 months. Store it between 5°C and 35°C, out of direct sunlight.
